Licenses: what you can do

Most open textbooks use a Creative Commons (CC) license. The license decides whether you can adopt the book, change it, or combine it with others.

The four conditions

Every CC license lets anyone copy and share the work for free. Each license adds some of these conditions:

BY: Attribution
Credit the original author. Every CC license includes this.
SA: ShareAlike
Anything you build from the work must use the same license.
NC: NonCommercial
No commercial use. Using it in your course is fine. A commercial publisher could not sell your version.
ND: NoDerivatives
Share it unchanged only. No editing, no remixing.

A book with an ND license can still be adopted. You can also pair it with separate materials you write yourself.

Combining texts into one book

When you blend material from two sources into a single text, the two licenses have to be compatible. Find one source's license on the left and the other's along the top.

Your finished book takes the most restrictive license among its sources. For example, if you blend a CC BY text with a CC BY-NC-SA text, your book must be CC BY-NC-SA.

Two exceptions to the chart:

  • Separate chapters: if each chapter stays distinct and keeps its own license, the licenses do not have to match. The chart applies only when you blend sources into one text.
  • Linking: you can always link to any source, whatever its license.

Remixing with AI

Remixing with AI does not change the license rules. If AI rewrites or combines chapters from a source, your version still has to follow that source's license. Keep a list of which sources fed which chapters, so your attribution is accurate.

Giving credit

For every source, give its Title, Author, Source (a link), and License, and say what you changed. For example:

Adapted from [Title] by [Author], [link], licensed under CC BY 4.0. Chapters 3 and 5 were condensed and reorganized.
